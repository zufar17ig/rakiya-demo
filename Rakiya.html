The problem

A structural mismatch, repeated every week.

Small businesses lose staff for a day. Students and informal workers want flexible income. Nothing connects them precisely, and nothing tracks what happens after.

Employers — shop owners, supermarkets, event organizers — have no fast, reliable way to fill a short-notice staffing gap. Their options: ask around, post in a WhatsApp group, or go without staff for the day.

Workers — students, school leavers, informal laborers — have no organized channel to discover nearby short-term jobs. They rely on word of mouth or scrolling unstructured WhatsApp and Facebook groups.

The channels that exist today only filter jobs at the district level — a unit that can span over 1,000 square kilometers. Even the closest competitor, SL Part Time, stops at the listing: once a worker is matched, attendance, payment, and outcome all move offline through phone calls, with nothing digitized on either side.

The solution

Four things no local alternative does today.

Rakiya doesn't invent informal work — it digitizes the full lifecycle of it: discovery, matching, and verified proof of completion.

Distance, not district

Geohash-based proximity matching ranks jobs within 10–15 km by real distance — work you can actually reach the same day.

Trust built into the flow

A worker's phone number stays hidden until the employer explicitly accepts — no unwanted contact before a match is real.

Two-sided verified proof

Both sides confirm what actually happened — payment received, work quality rated — building a portfolio no competitor tracks in either direction.

Accessible by design

Phone-number-only sign-in, a multilingual interface, and a WhatsApp-adjacent posting path — built for users who won't install another app.

How it works

One loop, two sides.

Every job moves through the same lifecycle — posted, matched, worked, and mutually confirmed — whichever side you're on.

As a worker

01

Discover nearby jobs

See jobs ranked by real distance from where you are, not a district-wide list.

02

Apply in one tap

Your portfolio and rating go with the application — your number doesn't, yet.

03

Get accepted

Only once accepted does the employer see your contact details, and you can chat.

04

Work, then confirm

Confirm you received the exact payment promised — cash, online, meals, or travel.

05

Build your record

Every closed job adds to a verified portfolio future employers can see.

As an employer

01

Post the gap

Role, pay, timing, and any physical requirements — a two-minute form.

02

Review real applicants

See ratings and completed job counts before you ever see a phone number.

03

Accept & reveal contact

One tap unlocks the worker's number and opens a chat thread to confirm the shift.

04

Get the work done

The worker shows up, does the job, and gets paid exactly as posted.

05

Rate & close

Rate the work to close the job and build your own reputation as a fair employer.

Go-to-market

Starting where the cold start problem is solvable.

The pilot launches in Gampaha town, within Gampaha District — the most populous district in Sri Lanka at roughly 2.43 million people, and the second-largest urban center in the district after Colombo.

It's dense enough to solve the two-sided cold start problem — enough workers and employers active at once — without competing on day one in Colombo, the country's most saturated commercial market.

Onboarding is manual and concierge-led at first: the founding team directly signs up local shops and recruits an early worker base from university and school-leaver networks, validated against a real, time-bound demand event before expanding.

Next: Colombo and other urban centers, once the model is proven at pilot scale.

Roadmap

Six stages, in order.

Stage 1 — Current

Hackathon build

Self-serve job posting, geohash matching within 10–15 km, phone-only auth, contact reveal on acceptance, mutual rating and proof flow — the core loop, working end to end.

Stage 2

Pre-pilot hardening

Close the two flagged pre-deployment gaps: production security rules and a real session/auth layer, plus a one-strike theft policy and a verified badge system.

Stage 3

Gampaha pilot

Live launch with a small, manually onboarded set of employers and workers — validating that the mutual rating flow actually gets completed by both sides.

Stage 4

Post-pilot refinement

Tighten the tag taxonomy against real postings, improve the WhatsApp bridge, and begin Sinhala interface work once speech-to-text accuracy is reassessed.

Stage 5

Expansion

Extend into Colombo and other urban centers. Full WhatsApp Business API replaces the sandbox; a recruitment-agency commercial tier becomes relevant.

Stage 6 — Not yet validated

Future enhancement

A B2G anonymized labor-market data licensing product, dependent on accumulated verified job history. Named honestly as a future idea, not a built feature.
